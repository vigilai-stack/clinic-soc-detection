# Many records outside the job

## What happened

One account opened, exported, or printed records their role does not use, for more patients than a mis-click explains.

## Why it matters here

This is the minimum-necessary problem in volume. Billing reading twenty clinical notes, or the front desk exporting a stack of notes, is not the job. Treat it as an incident even if the person says they were helping.

1. Call Avery Brooks (555-0142) and Sangeeta Sinha (555-0108). After hours, use 555-0190.
2. Disable the EHR account now.

4. Have Sam Patel confirm the workstation and, if the session is still going, disconnect that PC from the network.
5. Do not interview the employee until the account is disabled and the audit is copied out.

## What to isolate

The EHR account first. The workstation if exports, email, or a strange VPN session sit next to this alert. Leave the EHR application up.

## Evidence to keep

The audit extract, the role from the roster, the time the account was disabled, and any file name from an export or email alert.

## Breach-notification considerations

Start the written risk assessment the same day. Count the patients. Note whether the data stayed on the screen or left as a file. Counsel decides notice. The outer limit for individual notice is 60 days after discovery, and that is a ceiling, not the plan. If the count reaches 500 people in a state, media notice and notice to HHS are also in play. This clinic is small; count anyway. This checklist is not legal advice.

## False alarms

A wrong entry in the role list will flag a whole morning of legitimate work. Check config against the job description before you discipline anyone, and fix the config if the job really does need that record type.
