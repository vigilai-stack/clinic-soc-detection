# After-hours bulk chart viewing

## What happened

One workforce account opened clinical notes, labs, or medications for many patients while the clinic was closed. The account was not on the on-call roster.

## Why it matters here

Evening charting happens. The on-call nurse is supposed to open charts. A billing or front desk account opening a stack of clinical notes at 10 p.m. is a different event. The clinic's own rule is that billing works claims and insurance, not the note.

## What to do first

1. Call Avery Brooks (office manager, 555-0142) and Sangeeta Sinha (555-0108).
2. Disable the EHR account before anyone calls the employee, so a session cannot keep going during the conversation.
3. Check the VPN alert list for that user in the hour before the first chart.
4. Print or export the EHR audit for that user for the day onto a file the employee cannot delete. Store it with the privacy lead.
5. If the person is in the building, Avery walks over with a second person. Do not argue about motive in the hallway.

## What to isolate

The EHR account. The workstation only if the VPN or the logons also look wrong. Do not shut off the EHR for the rest of the clinic.

## Evidence to keep

The audit rows (user, time, patient MRN, record type, workstation, client address), the on-call roster for that date, and the time the account was disabled.

## Breach-notification considerations

Workforce snooping can be an impermissible use under the HIPAA Privacy Rule. Counsel decides whether it is a breach to notify. Your job tonight is the count of patients, the record types, and whether anything was exported or emailed. A view that stayed inside the EHR is a different conversation from a file that left. This checklist is not legal advice.

## False alarms

A clinician who stays late and charts a large panel can cross the count. If that keeps happening, put the late clinic on the roster or raise the number, and write down why. Do not add billing to the list of people who may read notes.
